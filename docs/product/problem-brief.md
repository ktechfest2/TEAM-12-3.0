# Clarion: problem brief

**The rumour speaks Yoruba. The correction speaks English.**

Weeks before the presidential election, a voice note drops into a family WhatsApp group: the election has been shifted. By evening it has crossed hundreds of groups. Amaka's mother hears it from a neighbour, in Yoruba, from someone she trusts. She decides there is no rush to collect her card. (Illustrative scenario.)

INEC does respond to rumours like this. When [a fake 2027 campaign timetable went viral in July 2026](https://africacheck.org/fact-checks/meta-programme-fact-checks/nigerias-electoral-commission-dismisses-viral-2027-general), the commission publicly said it had never issued it. But that denial was an English statement on official channels. Fact checkers warn that [local language misinformation will keep outpacing their work](https://www.icirnigeria.org/?p=270065).

## The weak link: the last mile of the truth

Nigeria does not lack official information. It lacks a way to deliver it. The correction gap is the distance between the moment a correction exists and the moment it reaches the voter who heard the lie, in their language and on their channel.

## Why now

INEC's chairman has said [the integrity of the 2027 ballot depends on the information environment](https://leadership.ng/?p=747001). The commission has [set up an AI unit and proposed an information integrity coalition](https://businessday.ng/politics/article/inec-sets-up-ai-unit-to-detect-deepfakes-protect-2027-elections/) and wants falsehoods [countered within the same news cycle](https://www.legit.ng/nigeria/1728629-just-inec-announces-what-undermine-2027-election/). The will is there. The delivery pipe is missing.

## What we are building

A claim comes in. Clarion finds what INEC actually said, a human reviewer approves the answer, and one tap sends it as SMS, voice notes in Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo and Pidgin, social posts, a radio spot and a town crier poster, each linked to the official source.
